🇧🇩 Bangladesh Wildlife Extinction Risk Forecast (2030)

A Data-Driven Biodiversity Risk Assessment Using Historical Data & Monte Carlo Simulation

📌 Project Overview

Bangladesh is home to a diverse range of wildlife species, but habitat loss, deforestation, climate change, and human activities are increasing pressure on its biodiversity.

This project aims to analyze the historical biodiversity status of Bangladesh and develop a model-based forecast of potential species extinction risks by 2030.

The analysis combines historical IUCN Red List assessments, forest-cover data, and Monte Carlo simulation to explore possible future biodiversity outcomes.

«Important: The 2030 results represent model-based scenarios, not confirmed or observed extinctions.»

🎯 Research Objectives

- Analyze Bangladesh's historical wildlife conservation status.
- Identify threatened species groups.
- Examine long-term forest-cover loss.
- Estimate potential extinction risks by 2030.
- Explore uncertainty through Monte Carlo simulation.
- Highlight conservation priorities.

Understanding the Forecast

The projected figure is a model-generated scenario based on historical information and assumptions.

It should not be interpreted as a confirmed prediction or an official government estimate.

⚠️ Limitations & Transparency

This research has several limitations:

- Comprehensive annual species-level data for 2010–2026 are not publicly available.
- Bangladesh's historical national Red List assessments are limited.
- The 2030 forecast depends on modeling assumptions.
- Monte Carlo simulation represents uncertainty but does not eliminate data limitations.
- New Red List assessments may produce different results.

The new national Red List assessment process initiated in 2026 may provide opportunities to validate and improve future forecasts.
