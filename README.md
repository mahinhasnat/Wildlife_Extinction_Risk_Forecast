 Bangladesh Wildlife Extinction Risk Forecast (2030)

A Research-Based Analysis of Wildlife, Biodiversity & Forest Loss in Bangladesh

---

📌 Research Overview

How many animal species could Bangladesh lose forever by 2030? I analyzed the data to find out.

Over the past few days, I have conducted a research-based analysis of Bangladesh's wildlife and forest ecosystems. For this research, I used the IUCN Bangladesh Red List (2000 and 2015), Forest Department data, and satellite data from Global Forest Watch.

I then conducted 20,000 Monte Carlo simulations to develop a risk forecast for 2030.

🌲 Forest Cover Analysis

Between 2001 and 2024, Bangladesh lost approximately 261,746 hectares of tree cover.

This represents approximately 13.5% of the total tree cover recorded in 2000.

In 2017 alone, the country lost approximately 28,000 hectares of tree cover.

📉 My 2030 Forecast

According to my model, an average of approximately 23 additional species could become nationally extinct in Bangladesh between 2015 and 2030.

90% uncertainty interval: 5–44 species.

The groups facing the highest potential risks include:

- Butterflies
- Mammals
- Freshwater fish

⚠️ Research Limitations & Transparency

I want to be transparent about the limitations of this research.

Annual species-level data covering 2010–2026 are not publicly available in Bangladesh. Only two official national assessment lists are available (2000 and 2015), and work on a new Red List began in 2026.

Therefore, my 2030 figures are not observed outcomes but model-based scenarios.

The results depend significantly on the assumptions used in the model. That is why I have included uncertainty bounds alongside the projected figures.

Once the new Red List is published, the actual accuracy of my forecast can be evaluated.
